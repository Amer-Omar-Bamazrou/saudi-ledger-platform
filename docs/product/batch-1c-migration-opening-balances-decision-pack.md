## 1. Executive summary

1. **Historical AR/AP: detailed items, as the product default — an aggregate opening balance per party is refused, not offered as an alternative.** The D-4 model (payments → explicit allocations → invoices; credit notes → applications; statements rebuilt from events; ageing from invoice due dates) has no place for a party-level lump: it cannot be allocated to, aged, or reconciled per document, and it would have to be represented as *a document anyway* (a synthetic "opening balance invoice") — which is the fake-history shape the migration principle forbids. A hybrid is not needed: a party whose old system tracked only a balance imports **one open item per unknown composition, dated, referenced to the source balance and marked as such** — still an item, still allocatable, honestly labelled. `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION`, built on `ACCOUNTING PRACTICE` (open-item accounting; IFRS 15/IAS 32 keep receivable and cash separate; every product studied enters open items individually).
2. **ZATCA boundary: an imported historical invoice is accounting evidence of a receivable, never a Saudi Ledger tax invoice.** It gets no ICV, no hash, no QR, no cryptographic stamp, is never cleared/reported, and never enters a VAT return of the new system. The obligation to *generate* an electronic invoice attaches to a tax invoice that *must be issued* for a supply (E-Invoicing Implementation Resolution, Seventh 1; VAT IR Art. 53) — a supply invoiced by the previous system was already invoiced, and re-issuing it would be a second tax invoice for one supply. `AUTHORITATIVE REQUIREMENT` for the generation obligation and the return's period basis; the accountant's pending confirmation that the platform's opening AR items are represented exactly this way is **preserved as the gate** (§9 Q1). Where the previous system was not e-invoicing-compliant (a business below the thresholds, or one that failed), the platform still does nothing: a compliance failure in the old system is not cured by re-issuing in the new one, and the platform has no authority to decide it. `No authoritative source located` on a "migration re-issuance" concept — none exists in the texts read, and none is inferred.
3. **Opening journal date: the day before the cutover date, in the last period of the previous system, which is then closed.** Cutover = the first day the company operates in Saudi Ledger; opening balances are the previous system's *closing* position at the end of the prior day. Posting them on cutover day itself would put a balance-sheet snapshot and the first day's real movements into the same period, so a trial balance "at cutover − 1" could not be produced and the first month's movement would include the opening. No special period is needed: the existing month lock closes the opening month after the reconciliation. `ACCOUNTING PRACTICE` (IFRS 1's opening statement of financial position "at the date of transition"; Xero's "day before the conversion date"; ERPNext/Zoho "as at the day before / start date") + `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION` on the lock.
4. **Reconciliation: four equalities are blockers, one is a warning.** Imported TB = posted opening journal = ledger read-back (per account, to the halala); Σ imported AR items (per customer) = AR subledger = GL AR(party); the same for AP; each bank's imported closing balance = its GL leaf opening = the reconciliation starting balance. Blocking. `OPENING_BALANCE_EQUITY` ≠ 0 after commit is *expected* and only *warns* until the accountant's clearing journal. `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION`, matching D-1's confirmed reconciliation.
5. **Source-row identity: `(company, source_system, source_id)` is the identity; `(company, document_type, source_document_number)` is the second, independent uniqueness; amount/date/party are validation, never identity.** Idempotency by a per-batch key plus the two unique constraints — a retry of a partly failed batch re-runs against the same identities and cannot double-post. `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION`.

**Verdict (§12): BATCH 1C — REQUIRES ACCOUNTANT DECISION** — one gate (the non-tax-invoice representation of opening AR/AP items) is still pending and it decides the ZATCA/VAT boundary; one further question (the mid-year year-to-date result) follows from the retained-earnings fact the repository revealed. The third question originally listed — a party balance with no document detail — was resolved by reading Odoo and ERPNext source (§13.2) and is a product decision.

## 3. Detailed historical items vs aggregate opening balances

### The two options, and what each does to every reader

| Consequence | Option A — detailed items (one row per open invoice / bill, original number, original date, original due date, open amount) | Option B — aggregate opening balance per party (one figure, e.g. "Rawabi owes 25,000") |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AR ageing** | Ages from each item's original due date — the buckets are true from day one (`reports.service.arAging` reads `due_date` per item). | No due date exists. Either the whole 25,000 sits in "current" (false), or a synthetic due date is invented (false), or ageing is suppressed for migrated balances until they are collected (a report that lies by omission for months). |
| **Customer statement** | The opening position lists the items; later receipts, allocations and credits attach to them; the running balance is meaningful. | One opening line; every later receipt is "against the balance", so the statement cannot say *which* invoice is paid or disputed. The statement event model (`customerStatement.service`) has no kind for "balance". |
| **Payment allocation** | `POST /payments … allocations[{invoiceId, amount}]` works unchanged; partial payments are partial allocations; the unallocated remainder is a deposit (D-4, unchanged). | There is nothing to allocate to. To make a receipt reduce the balance the platform must invent a target — a synthetic invoice row — which IS Option A with a worse name. The alternative (a receipt that reduces "opening AR" without a document) needs a second settlement path beside `payment_allocations`, forbidden by "one writer per effect". |
| **Partial payments / disputes** | An item can be part-paid, credited, or left open; collections can chase a named invoice. | Only a balance can be part-paid; a dispute about one invoice cannot be expressed. |
| **Credit application** | A credit note issued in the new system (a real tax document, Art. 54(4) references its original) can be applied against the migrated item it corrects **only if that item exists as a document**. | A credit note against "the opening balance" has no original to reference — it cannot be issued correctly under Art. 54(4). |
| **Historical audit trail** | Each item carries its source system, source id and original number; the previous system's ledger can be walked line by line. | The composition of 25,000 exists only outside the platform. |
| **GL reconciliation** | AR(party) GL lines in the opening journal = Σ items per party = subledger — the existing three-way equality per customer holds and the sweep can assert it (with an opening rule beside RULE-J). | GL AR(party) = one figure; the subledger (`invoices`) is empty for the party; the sweep's AR-by-customer check has nothing to compare and the invariant "AR GL = customer AR subledger" is silently vacuous for every migrated party. |
| **Migration complexity** | More rows to import (one per open item) and a per-party sub-total check (Σ items = the party's balance = the control account). The tool must accept a file of open items. | Fewer rows. But it needs a second settlement path, a second statement kind, a second ageing rule and a second invariant exception — complexity moved from the import file into the accounting engine, permanently. |
| **Corrections** | A wrong item is corrected by a further item or a credit application, or by reversing the migration and re-running (D-1); never edited. | A wrong balance is a wrong GL figure with nothing beneath it to correct; the only correction is a journal against AR(party) — the manual-JE-to-AR shape the ERPNext comparison already flagged as the first thing a migrating bookkeeper does wrong. |

### Why a hybrid is not needed

The only case Option B seems to serve is *a party whose old system tracked no items* (a spreadsheet, a cash book, a balance carried from memory). That case is served inside Option A: the migration imports **one open item** for that party — amount = the balance, date = the last date the source can vouch for (or cutover − 1), due date = the same (so it ages as "current" honestly, or per the source's own terms), `source_document_number = the source balance reference`, and a marker `composition_unknown` shown on the statement and ageing. It is still a document: allocatable, creditable (a new credit note can reference it), reconcilable per party, correctable by a further item. The product needs one model and one set of readers; the "aggregate" case is a degenerate *input*, not a second *representation*. `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION`.

What Option A must **not** do: pretend the item is a tax invoice (§4); mint it a number from the C12 series (its number is the source's); post VAT for it (the VAT was reported by the previous system — the pending accountant confirmation, §9 Q1); give it `invoice_items` that fabricate a line composition the source did not provide (one line, "Opening item — migrated from <source>", amount only).

### What the products studied do (product-design evidence only)

| Product | Behaviour | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ERPNext | An *Opening Invoice Creation Tool* creates one invoice per unpaid customer invoice and one per unpaid supplier invoice, with posting date, due date and "legacy reference"; the user then runs AR/AP at the cutover date and compares totals and ageing to the source; the tool warns to exclude the same control-account balance from the opening journal ("avoid double counting"). | docs.frappe.io, *Opening Invoice Creation Tool* (fetched 2026-09-17) |

**Conclusion 3.** Detailed items as the single model; the aggregate case is one dated, marked item. `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION` on `ACCOUNTING PRACTICE`.

## 4. ZATCA / historical document boundary

**The question.** A migrated invoice row exists in `invoices`. Does it become a tax invoice of this platform, get an ICV / hash / QR / stamp, get cleared or reported, or enter a VAT return?

**What the primary texts say (read in `docs/zatca/specs/`, English translations; the Arabic prevails):**

| Point | Text | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What must be generated electronically | *Controls, Requirements, Technical Specifications and Procedural Rules* (Governor's Decision 62738 of 23/11/1443H, English), **Seventh (1)**: persons subject to the Regulation must "Generate all tax invoices and notes **that must be issued** within the timelines specified in the VAT law, in an electronic form". **First (5)**: the transactions in scope are "the cases for which tax invoices must be issued and set forth under Article (53) of the VAT Implementing Regulation" — supplies, exports, intra-GCC supplies, nominal supplies, advance payments. | The generation obligation attaches to a tax invoice that a supply *requires to be issued*. A supply invoiced by the previous system has had its tax invoice issued (or its failure is the previous system's); importing the accounting record of that receivable is not a case "for which a tax invoice must be issued". `AUTHORITATIVE REQUIREMENT` for the scope; the application to migration is a reading, not a stated rule. |
| Issuance timing | VAT IR (Eighth Ed., English) **Art. 53(1)(b)**: the tax invoice "must be issued no later than the fifteenth day of the month following the month in which the Supply took place". | A migrated invoice's supply took place before cutover; its issuance window closed then. Re-issuing it in the new system would be a second tax invoice for one supply, outside the window — an error, not a compliance step. |
| Counter, hash, sequence | Resolution **Third/C (3, 5, 7)** and **E (2)**: the UUID and sequential number, the hash chained to the previous invoice, and a tamper-resistant counter exist "for each Electronic Invoice or Electronic Note **generated by** such E-Invoice Generation Solution"; one sequence per unit. | Only documents this platform *generates* enter its ICV/hash chain. A migrated row is not generated here; giving it an ICV would insert a foreign document into the unit's sequence — the "second concurrent series" the C12 work already refused. |
| Deduction / reporting | Resolution **First (3)**: e-invoices generated per the Resolution and cleared/reported per the integration phase are the tax invoices considered for input-tax deduction. | A migrated *bill* is the supplier's tax invoice, already issued (electronically, if the supplier was in scope) by the supplier's system; the platform's copy is an accounting record of the payable, not the deduction evidence — that evidence is the supplier's document, retained under Art. 66. |
| The return's period | VAT IR **Tax Returns, para (2)**: the return discloses supplies, purchases and tax "in respect of the Tax Period to which a Tax Return relates"; filing per tax period (para 1). | Supplies of pre-cutover periods belong to pre-cutover returns, filed from the previous system. The new system's return for the first period must not include migrated items. `AUTHORITATIVE REQUIREMENT` for the period basis. |
| Records | VAT IR **Art. 66(1)(3)**: invoices, books, records kept six years from the end of the tax period; "original documents corroborating all entries in accounting books shall be maintained"; Resolution **Fifth**: e-invoices and their data kept per Art. 66. | The previous system's invoices, returns and closing trial balance are the corroborating documents of the opening journal and of every opening item; the migration must retain them (batch evidence — §7) but does not become their custodian. `AUTHORITATIVE REQUIREMENT` for retention. |

**What the texts do not say — `No authoritative source located`:**
- Neither the Regulation, the Resolution, the Detailed Guideline nor the FAQ material read describes a "migration", "system change", "opening balance" or "re-issuance" concept. The conclusion that migrated documents stay outside the e-invoicing chain is a reading of the *generation* obligation's scope, not a stated rule.
- Nothing read addresses a business whose previous system was non-compliant. The platform takes no position and re-issues nothing; that is the taxpayer's matter with the Authority.
- Whether a migrated invoice must carry the previous system's ICV/UUID/hash *as data* for the six-year record: not stated. The platform stores the source identifiers it is given (§7) and never treats them as its own.

**Therefore (the boundary, as a product rule):** a migrated item is `documentKind = opening` (or an equivalent marker) and is **refused** by: `invoices.approvable` (never submitted/approved; it enters the books through the opening journal, not through issuance), the ICV allocator, the hash chain, QR generation, `enqueueEInvoice`, the e-invoice archive, the VAT return readers (both the document-based return and the reconciliation view), and the invoice document renderer's "tax invoice" template (it may print as a *statement of an opening item*, never as a tax invoice). It is **accepted** by: the AR/AP subledger caches, ageing, the customer statement, payment allocation, credit-note application (as a target), the invariant sweep (with an explicit opening rule), and the audit trail. `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION`, fail-closed: a reader that has not been taught the marker must exclude by default rather than include — which means the marker is a *positive* flag readers must opt into, and the sweep asserts that no `opening` row ever has an ICV, a hash, a QR, an archive row or an outbox row.

**The pending gate, preserved.** The accountant has been asked whether opening AR items should be represented as non-tax-invoices with no ICV, no e-invoice and no VAT. This pack does not answer it; the reading above is the platform's *proposed* representation, consistent with that question's premise, and it is built only when the answer arrives (§9 Q1). If the answer were "no" (e.g. the accountant wants VAT-bearing opening items for some reason), the model in §3 is unchanged and the boundary above is what changes — which is why the gate blocks implementation but not the data-model design.

## 5. Opening journal date / period

**Definitions.** *Cutover date* = the first business day on which the company records new transactions in Saudi Ledger. *Opening date* = the date the opening journal carries = **cutover − 1 day** (the last day of the previous system). Both are stored on the migration record; nothing else in the product infers one from the other.

**Why cutover − 1 and not cutover day.** `ACCOUNTING PRACTICE`: an opening statement of financial position is a snapshot "at the date of transition" (IFRS 1 ¶6–11: an opening IFRS statement of financial position at the date of transition; adjustments recognised directly in retained earnings or another category of equity — IFRS 1 is about a change of *framework*, not of *system*, so it is an analogy for the mechanics, not an authority for the migration itself). The previous system's closing balances are *its* closing position at the end of its last day; posting them at the end of that day means (a) a trial balance "as at opening date" reproduces the previous system's closing trial balance exactly — the first reconciliation in §6 is a report, not a computation; (b) the first live period contains only live movements, so its movement column, its VAT return and its cash-flow statement are free of the opening; (c) a bank reconciliation for the first live month starts from the statement's opening balance on cutover day, which equals the GL leaf after the opening journal. Vendor practice agrees (Xero: "the day before you start using Xero"; Zoho: the "Opening Balance Date" is the start date and pre-date transactions are folded into it) — evidence, not authority. `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION` to adopt cutover − 1.

**Interaction with periods and locks.**
- The opening month (the month containing cutover − 1) is the last month of the previous system. After the reconciliation (§6) agrees, the migration workflow **closes that month with the existing `period_locks` mechanism** (a normal lock, audited, naming the migration). No special period type is created: the existing lock already refuses every writer through `checkPeriodOpen`, which is exactly the property wanted — nothing but the migration's own reversal (which unlocks, reverses, and re-locks as one audited act) may post into it. `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION`; the alternative (a "period 0" or a special opening state) was rejected because every reader would need to learn it and the month lock already provides the refusal.
- **Migrated items keep their original dates** (issue date, due date) even though those dates fall inside the now-locked opening month or earlier: the lock governs *posting* (the journal date), and the opening journal is dated on the opening date. The items themselves post no journal of their own — their AR/AP lines are inside the opening journal. So the original dates are free to be true. `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION` consistent with "do not create an artificial boundary that changes historical dates".
- **Cutover day itself** is an ordinary open day: the first real invoice, receipt or bank row is dated on or after it. A migrated bank statement row dated before cutover is *evidence* (attached to the bank's opening balance, §6), not an imported transaction — importing it would double the opening.
- **A fiscal year that does not start at cutover** (most migrations happen mid-year): the opening journal on cutover − 1 carries the previous system's *year-to-date* P&L as part of the balance sheet — see the retained-earnings note below. Reports for the partial year show the platform's own P&L from cutover; the previous system's statements cover the earlier part. Any comparative or full-year figure is a two-source figure and the product should say so rather than merge. `ACCOUNTING PRACTICE` (IAS 1 comparatives are the entity's, not the software's; nothing forces a system to hold pre-migration P&L detail).

**The retained-earnings consequence (a current-state fact that Batch 1C must not ignore).** Retained earnings on this platform is *computed* from P&L lines and there is no stored equity account for it (§2). A migrated company's prior retained earnings therefore cannot be "the computed figure" — it is an imported *balance* and needs a **real, postable equity account** to land on when OBE is cleared. The accountant's decision ("cleared to retained earnings by a dated journal") presupposes that account exists. Batch 1C must add a system equity account for retained earnings (and the balance sheet must present *stored* retained earnings + *computed current-period result* as two lines, not one), or the clearing journal has nowhere to go. This is an engineering prerequisite, not a new accounting question — it follows from the decision already taken. `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION`. (Whether a mid-year cutover's year-to-date result is imported as P&L balances or folded into opening retained earnings is §9 Q2; Odoo and ERPNext diverge on it — §13.4.)

## 6. Three-way reconciliation

The migration is **not complete** until every blocking equality holds, per company, to the halala, and the result is stored on the migration record as evidence (Art. 66(3)(c) — the corroborating document of the opening entries is the source trial balance and this reconciliation).

| # | Equality | Compared how | Blocks? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | **Imported trial balance = posted opening journal = ledger read-back**, per account | the import file's Dr/Cr per mapped account ↔ the opening journal's lines per account ↔ `accountSummary`/trial balance as at the opening date | **Blocks** commit (D-1, confirmed). A mismatch is a mapping or arithmetic error and nothing is posted. |
| R2 | **Σ imported AR items per customer = AR subledger per customer = GL AR(party) per customer**; and Σ over customers = the AR control line of R1 | the item file ↔ `invoices` opening rows (outstanding) ↔ the opening journal's AR lines carrying `customer_id` | **Blocks**. This is what makes the ageing, the statement and the D-4 allocation model true from day one; without it the sweep's AR-by-customer invariant is vacuous. |
| R3 | **Σ imported AP items per supplier = AP subledger per supplier = GL AP(party) per supplier**; Σ = the AP control line | the bill file ↔ `bills` opening rows ↔ opening journal AP(vendor) lines | **Blocks**, same reason. |
| R4 | **Imported bank closing balance (per bank) = the bank's GL leaf balance at the opening date = the bank reconciliation's starting balance** | the stated closing balance (with the statement page as evidence) ↔ the leaf's balance after the opening journal ↔ the figure the future reconciliation record starts from | **Blocks**. The typed `bank_accounts.opening_balance` becomes the migration's *input* and is never again a balance in its own right (G2). |
| R5 | **Assets = Liabilities + Equity** on the opening date, with `OPENING_BALANCE_EQUITY` as the balancing equity line | the balance sheet as at the opening date | Holds by construction when R1 holds (Dr = Cr). Asserted anyway. |
| R6 | **`OPENING_BALANCE_EQUITY` = the imported TB's equity difference; = 0 after clearing** | the landing account's balance | **Warns**, never blocks: a non-zero balance after commit is the expected state until the accountant posts the clearing journal (decided). The flag stays visible on the trial balance, balance sheet and migration record until it is 0. |
| R7 | **No opening item carries an ICV, hash, QR, archive row or outbox row; no opening item is in any VAT return** | the sweep | **Blocks** (a violation means a reader failed closed — a defect, not data). |
| R8 | **Every opening AR/AP item has a corresponding party line in the opening journal, and no party line lacks an item** | both directions (the "when a map replaces a map, assert both directions" rule) | **Blocks**. |

**Dry-run first, always.** The whole set is computed on a preview with zero writes; commit re-computes it inside the transaction and refuses on any difference from the preview (the `cash_cutover_runs` pattern). `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION`.

## 7. Source row identity / idempotency

**Identity (deterministic, never fuzzy).**

| Row kind | Identity (unique, per company) | Second uniqueness | Validation only (never identity) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening TB line | `(company, batch, account_ref)` — one line per source account per batch | `(company, opening_date, mapped_account_id)` after mapping — two source accounts may not map to one platform account without an explicit merge marker | amount |
| Opening AR item | `(company, source_system, source_id)` where `source_id` is the previous system's stable identifier of the document (its primary key or its document number when that is the only stable id) | `(company, document_type, invoice_number)` — the existing `invoices_company_number_unq` | customer, date, due date, amount, currency (SAR only — the platform refuses others) |
| Opening AP item | same, with `bill_number` and the supplier | `(company, bill_number)` — the existing `bills_company_number_unq` (a supplier's number may collide across suppliers; the migration prefixes with the supplier reference when it does, and says so) | supplier, dates, amount |
| Bank opening | `(company, bank_account_id, opening_date)` | — | closing balance, statement evidence reference |
| Batch | `(company, batch_number)`; content hash of the input files stored | — | — |

**Rules.**
1. A row without a `source_id` is refused when the source system is named; a row with a `source_id` that already exists for the company is a duplicate and is refused **before** anything is written (never silently skipped and never merged — the statement-import "skip and report" behaviour is right for statements and wrong here, because an opening item skipped is a receivable lost).
2. Amount, date and party are checked against the existing row when a `source_id` collides (a retry with a changed amount is an error, not an update).
3. `source_system` is a stated string per batch ("PreviousERP", "Excel", "Qoyod export"…); rows of one batch share it; two batches may not both name the same `source_id` for the same company.
4. The batch carries an idempotency key (the client's), the content hash of its files, and its own preview result; commit with the same key returns the same batch; commit with a different key over overlapping identities is refused by rule 1.
5. Original document numbers are **kept as the document's number** and shown wherever the platform shows a number (statement, ageing, allocation dialog), with the source system beside them; the platform never renumbers a migrated document, and the C12 counter never sees them.
6. **No cross-company identity.** All identities are scoped by `company_id`; a batch names one company; RLS scopes the organisation; the FK-outside-RLS lesson applies — every FK a migration row carries (customer, vendor, bank, account) is validated against the batch's company in the service, not only by the database.

**What is refused as identity:** amount-only, nearest-date, name similarity, "same customer and same month". These remain what they are for matching (evidence that partitions candidates), never identity. `PRODUCT DESIGN DECISION`.

### 13.2 Q2 — resolved as a product decision; proceeding

**SAUDI LEDGER PRODUCT DECISION.** A party whose previous system gives only a balance imports as **one opening item** (an `opening` invoice or bill row): amount = the balance; number = the source's balance reference (e.g. `OB-<source>-<party>`), never a C12 number; issue date = the date the source vouches for the balance, else the opening date (cutover − 1); due date = the same unless the source states terms (so it ages honestly as "current" at cutover rather than under an invented due date); one amount-only line "Opening balance — composition not available from <source>"; marker `composition_unknown` shown on the statement, the ageing row and the allocation dialog. It is allocatable, creditable (a new credit note can reference it) and reconcilable per party exactly like a detailed item. This is the representation both reference implementations use (an opening invoice per row in ERPNext; a reconcilable receivable line per partner in Odoo), it carries no VAT/ZATCA consequence beyond Q1's, and it changes no accounting meaning — so it is not sent to the accountant. Recorded in §3 ("the aggregate case is a degenerate input") and now closed.

### 13.3 Q1 — what ERPNext already settles as design, and what stays his

ERPNext's `is_opening` flag and Opening Invoice Creation Tool answer the *representation*: one document per open item; original number as the document id; original posting and due dates; a single amount-only line; no taxes computed by the tool; flagged so reports treat it as opening and posting refuses it after the books close; ordinary in the payment ledger. Saudi Ledger adopts that shape (§3, §4) with two deliberate differences, both engineering: (1) the item's AR/AP GL line is written by the **opening journal** (one entry, party on the line, `OPENING_BALANCE_EQUITY` on the far side) rather than by the item posting its own entry against a temporary account — one writer per effect, and the RULE-J sweep then reconciles the item to the journal line instead of to a `GL-<number>` entry; (2) the marker is refused by issuance, the ICV/hash chain, QR, the outbox, the archive and the VAT-return readers (§4), which ERPNext has no equivalent of because it has no e-invoicing chain in this module. **What stays the accountant's:** whether the item carries a VAT amount at all and its exclusion from the platform's returns — the pending question, unchanged.

(B) Fold YTD result into opening retained earnings (a stored equity account): the opening journal is balance-sheet only (ERPNext's rule); the platform's income statement shows post-cutover activity; the cutover year is read from two systems and the product says so.
- Recommended engineering default — None that avoids the policy: the choice IS the policy. Until answered, the opening journal REFUSES P&L accounts (the stricter of the two, reversible by lifting a check; B works under it, A does not). ACCOUNTANT DECISION REQUIRED.
- Exact question for the accountant — "For a company that moves to Saudi Ledger mid-year: do you want its year-to-date profit or loss brought in as income and expense account balances (so our income statement shows the whole year), or as a single opening retained-earnings figure (so our income statement starts at the cutover and the year's statements are read from both systems)? Either is buildable; it changes which statement the Zakat base is read from."
- Implementation impact — Blocks the opening-journal validator's account-type rule and the balance-sheet presentation of stored retained earnings vs current-period result. Nothing else in Batch 1C waits on it.

## 14.1 Question 1 — historical AR/AP items: opening accounting items, or new Saudi Ledger tax invoices?

### 14.1.1 The five things that must not be collapsed

| Concept | What it is | Who created it | What law attaches to it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Historical source-system document** | The tax invoice the previous system issued for a supply before cutover | the supplier, then, in that system | VAT Law Arts. 2, 18 (tax on the supply, at the rate of the date of supply); IR Art. 53 (issuance, content, 15-day window); the e-invoicing obligations of the person who issued it, then |
| **Imported accounting record** | Saudi Ledger's row stating "this document is still owed SAR X at cutover" | the migration, now | IR Art. 66(1)(3)(c): books, records and the documents corroborating every entry, kept six years; nothing else |
| **New Saudi Ledger tax invoice** | A document this platform issues for a supply, with a sequential number, an issue date, VAT | the supplier, now, in this system | IR Art. 53(5); the Resolution's generation/chain/QR/stamp requirements for documents *generated by* this solution |
| **New ZATCA e-invoice** | The XML this platform generates, chains (ICV, PIH), stamps and clears/reports | this solution unit | Resolution Third/C, Third/E, Sixth |
| **New VAT event** | A supply, or consideration received before a supply, in a tax period | the taxable person's activity | VAT Law Arts. 2, 18, 24; IR Art. 20; the return for that period |

The question is whether the second thing must, or may, become the third, fourth or fifth. Each sub-question below is answered on its own evidence.

### 14.1.2 What triggers the invoice / e-invoice obligation (1C)

| Provision | Text (as read) | What it establishes | Addresses migration? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| VAT Law Art. 2 | "Tax shall be imposed on the import and supply of goods and services…" | Tax attaches to the **supply**. | No — general rule |
| VAT Law Art. 18 | "The tax rate due shall be the rate applicable on the date of supply or the date of import." | The tax is fixed by the **date of supply**, not by any later record. | No |
| VAT Law Art. 21(1) | "If an invoice is issued or payment is made … prior to the date this Law enters into force or prior to the registration date, and the supply takes place after such date, tax shall be due on the date of supply." | The Law itself separates *invoice issued* from *tax due* — an invoice is not the taxable event; the supply is. (A transitional rule, cited for its principle, not its facts.) | No |
| VAT Law Art. 1 (definition) | "Tax Invoice: An invoice issued in respect of taxable supplies in accordance with the requirements provided for in the Law and Regulations." | A tax invoice is defined by the **supply it is issued for**. | No |
| IR Art. 53(1)(a)(b) | A tax invoice must be issued where the taxable person makes a taxable supply (a)(1) or receives consideration before a supply (a)(2); "In all cases … the Tax Invoice must be issued no later than the fifteenth day of the month following the month in which the Supply took place." | The issuance obligation's triggers are enumerated: **a supply, or an advance**. | IR Art. 53(5)(a)(b) | A tax invoice includes "the date of issue" and "a sequential number which uniquely identifies the Tax Invoice". | The number identifies **the tax invoice**, i.e. the document issued for the supply. | No |
| IR Art. 20 | Date of supply / tax due date in specific circumstances (continuous supplies: earliest of due date, payment, invoice). | Tax due dates are events of supply, payment or invoicing — never a records event. | No |
| Resolution First (5) | Transactions subject to Art. 3 of the E-Invoicing Regulation "include the cases for which tax invoices must be issued and set forth under Article (53) of the VAT Implementing Regulation" — supplies, exports, intra-GCC supplies, nominal supplies, advance payments. | The e-invoicing scope is **the same set of events** as the tax-invoice obligation. | No |
| Resolution Seventh (1) | Persons subject must "Generate all tax invoices and notes **that must be issued** within the timelines specified in the VAT law, in an electronic form". | The generation obligation attaches to a tax invoice that a supply requires; it does not create invoices of its own. | No |
| Resolution Third/C (3)(5)(7); Third/E (2) | UUID and sequential number, hash chained to the previous invoice, and a tamper-resistant counter, "for each Electronic Invoice or Electronic Note **generated by** such E-Invoice Generation Solution"; one sequence per unit. | ICV / hash / QR / stamp apply to what **this** solution generates. | No |

| Detailed Guideline v2, FAQ "Can I submit the same invoice twice?" | "No, ZATCA does not allow the same document to be submitted twice." | A supply already invoiced and reported through the previous solution must not be submitted again. | No (it addresses duplicate submission, not migration) |

**Finding (1C).** The trigger is **the taxable supply** (or consideration received before it). It is not the creation of an invoice, not the use of an accounting system, and not the movement of records between systems. Every text lists its triggers positively; none of them is a migration. `AUTHORITATIVE REQUIREMENT` for the trigger. And, stated as required: **No authoritative ZATCA source located that specifically addresses migration/re-issuance of historical invoices.** The conclusion that a migration is not a trigger is therefore a classification of the migration against the enumerated triggers — a direct application of the positive rule — not an inference from the texts' silence. Confidence in the application: high; it is nevertheless a reading, and it goes to the accountant for sign-off because it turns on VAT and ZATCA (protocol §7).

### 14.1.3 VAT return treatment (1D)

| Provision | Text (as read) | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VAT Law Art. 24(1)(2) | "The Regulations shall determine the tax period for which a taxable person must file a tax return." "A taxable person must file a tax return with the Authority for each tax period…" | Returns are per **tax period**. |
| IR Tax Returns para (2) | The return discloses "in respect of the Tax Period to which a Tax Return relates: (a) the total value of all Supplies … and the total Output Tax on those Supplies; (b) the total value of all Goods and services supplied to the Taxable Person, and the total deductible Input Tax…" | The return's content is **the period's supplies and purchases**. |
| VAT Law Art. 24(3); Art. 25; IR Art. 63 (as amended — read for D-5) | An error in a filed return is corrected by the return-correction procedure. | A supply that was wrongly reported (or not reported) in a **prior** period is a **correction of that period's return**, never a new event in a later one. |

**Finding (1D).** Importing the outstanding balance of an invoice for a supply that occurred before cutover creates **no VAT reporting event**: the supply's output tax was due in the supply's period and belongs to that period's return, filed from the previous system; the later **settlement** of the receivable is a cash event with no VAT content (the tax was on the supply, VAT Law Art. 2); and the imported record is a record under Art. 66. `AUTHORITATIVE REQUIREMENT` for the period basis and the correction route; the application to an imported balance is, again, a classification against positive rules. **Facts that matter:** (i) that the original supply was reported in the previous system's return for its period — if it was not, the remedy is Art. 63's correction of *that* return, which is a separate tax workflow, not a migration step; (ii) that the item is a settled-supply receivable and not an **advance** — consideration received before a supply is itself a tax point (IR Art. 53(1)(a)(2); Resolution First (5)(E)), so a customer *deposit* migrated at cutover carries the VAT position the previous system gave it and any *later* supply against it is a new event in this system; (iii) the **VAT balances** themselves (output tax payable / input tax receivable at cutover) are opening balance-sheet balances of the previous period's returns and are imported as such — they are not re-derived from the imported items. **No authoritative source located** that names migration; none was needed to reach the period rule.

| IFRS 9 ¶3.1.1 | "An entity shall recognise a financial asset or a financial liability in its statement of financial position when, and only when, the entity becomes party to the contractual provisions of the instrument." | The receivable exists because of the contract with the customer, not because of the system holding it; at cutover the entity already holds it, so the new books must **carry** it — recognition happened at the supply. `ACCOUNTING STANDARD`. |

Separation kept: the standards establish *recognition, presentation and traceability*; they establish **nothing** about ZATCA or VAT, and are not used to.

### 14.2.1 What the accounting standards establish (2A)

## 15.2 Verification of the regulatory claims

### A. The 12-month input-VAT rule — VERIFIED (with one limit)

Source: VAT Implementing Regulations, Eighth Edition English (repo copy), **Article 40 "Adjustment to value of a Supply"**, paragraphs 7–12 (pp. 29–30 of the file). Read for this pass:

| Para | Text (as read) | Establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **40(10)** | "Any Taxable Person who has deducted Input Tax in respect of a Supply received by that Person, but has failed to make payment in full after a period of twelve months from the date of Supply, must reduce the Input Tax deduction by the amount of Tax calculated on the Consideration not paid at that date." | **Trigger:** twelve months from the **date of supply** without full payment. **Affected party:** the buyer who deducted. **VAT treatment:** the deduction is **reduced** by the tax on the unpaid consideration. **Timing:** measured "at that date" (the twelve-month point). **Conditions:** applies where input tax was deducted and consideration is unpaid; no other condition stated. |
| **40(11)** | "In cases where an adjustment to Input Tax is made under the tenth Paragraph … and the Taxable Person subsequently makes payment of the Consideration, the Input Tax deduction may be increased correspondingly to reflect the Tax calculated on the Consideration paid." | **Re-claim** on later payment, to the extent paid. |
| **40(8)** | "A person using the cash accounting basis described in Article forty-six … must not make any adjustment for non-payment prescribed in the seventh Paragraph…" | The cash-basis exclusion is written for the **seller's** para 7; para 10 has no stated exclusion (a cash-basis buyer deducts only on payment anyway). |
| **40(7)** | The **seller** may reduce output tax for unpaid consideration only when: tax was reported and paid; the customer is not a related person; **at least twelve months** have passed from the supply; a certified accountant's certificate of write-off is held; and, above SAR 100,000 unpaid, formal legal recovery has been attempted. **40(9)**: later receipt makes the tax payable again and "a new Tax invoice must be issued to reflect the additional amount received." | The seller's relief is **optional, conditional and evidenced**; not a mirror of the buyer's mandatory reduction. |
| **40(12)** | Adjustments are subject to the time limits of the Law and Regulations. | — |

**Accountant's claim: CONFIRMED** — `AUTHORITATIVE REQUIREMENT` (Art. 40(10)–(11)). **Does migration change the rule?** No: the twelve months run from the **date of supply**, which is the original document's date — so a migrated open bill carries its 12-month clock with it, and the original supply date **must** be preserved for the rule to be applied. **Required evidence:** the unpaid amount at the twelve-month date and the payment later made (the platform's AP open item and allocation records are exactly that). **Not established by the text read:** (i) the *return* in which the reduction is reported (the Eighth Edition says "at that date"; the reporting period is not spelled out), and (ii) the **accounting account** for the reversed deduction (the liability to the supplier is unchanged; only the deductible input tax falls) — an asset "input VAT suspended — unpaid > 12 months" or an expense is an accounting-treatment choice. **Limitation:** the Arabic Tenth Edition (April 2025) was not re-read for Article 40. **Saudi Ledger current behaviour:** the rule is **not implemented for any bill** (no reader of `bills.date` computes a twelve-month adjustment) — a gap outside Batch 1C's migration scope, recorded as an implementation consequence (§15.7).

**What those sources establish (`AUTHORITATIVE REQUIREMENT` / official artefact):**
- The sequence is **per unit**: "The Compliant E-Invoice solution must not be able to generate more than one sequence of Electronic Invoices and Electronic Notes issued through each of the units within the E-Invoice Solution used. … a Unit is the key component … that generates the tamper-proofing features of the sequence … by inserting a Hash … in addition to including the Hash of the previous Electronic Invoice" (Resolution Third/E (2)); "all invoices generated by an E-Invoice Solution Unit are linked using 'Previous Invoice Hash' value into a single chain" (Detailed Guideline §5.6).
- The hash chains **generated** documents: "generate a Hash for each generated Electronic Invoice … within the sequence … The Hash of the Electronic Invoice is then embedded in the next Electronic Invoice in the sequence" (Third/C (5)); the counter "cannot be reset or reformatted" (Third/C (7)).
- The PIH is the hash of the **immediately preceding generated document on that unit**, rejected documents included (Technical Guideline FAQ: "Document 3 should have its Previous Invoice Hash as the hash of Document 2 even though it was rejected"; and after a reporting rejection "Previous Invoice Hash will be based on immediately preceding document").
- A **new EGS unit is onboarded with its own CSID** through the Fatoora portal ("Onboard new solution unit/device", Technical Guideline §3.2–3.3); CSIDs are renewed or revoked per unit.
- **The first document of a chain carries the genesis PIH** — the SDK ships it as `Data/PIH/pih.txt`: `NWZlY2ViNjZmZmM4NmYzOGQ5NTI3ODZjNmQ2OTZjNzljMmRiYzIzOWRkNGU5MWI0NjcyOWQ3M2EyN2ZiNTdlOQ==` (Base64 of the hex SHA-256 of "0"), and the SDK validates KSA-13's format against it. This is an **official ZATCA artefact**, and it is what Saudi Ledger already uses for a company's first document (`services/einvoice/crypto/invoiceHash.ts`, sourced from the SDK).

**What they do NOT establish:**
- Whether a taxpayer **replacing** one Phase-2 solution with another is, from ZATCA's side, starting a **new unit's chain** (genesis PIH) or **continuing the taxpayer's chain** (the old solution's last hash). The Resolution defines the chain per *unit*; the Technical Guideline says ZATCA's platform "will be storing and tracking the hash" of documents — but **not whether that tracking is per unit or per taxpayer**, nor what it expects as the first PIH of a newly onboarded unit for a taxpayer with prior history.
- Whether the treatment differs for standard tax invoices, simplified tax invoices, credit notes or debit notes — no text distinguishes; all four are "Electronic Invoices and Electronic Notes" in one sequence per unit.
- Any **migration / system-change procedure** — none exists in the texts read. Explicitly: **no authoritative ZATCA source located that addresses changing e-invoicing solutions or the first PIH after such a change.**
- Old-system invoices: nothing states their status after a change beyond the general record-keeping duty (Resolution Fifth; VAT IR Art. 66) — they remain the taxpayer's records; whether they are "outside the new chain" is a consequence of the per-unit definition, not a stated rule.

**Why neither value can be asserted safely:**
- **`PIH = genesis`** rests on reading "new solution = new unit"; if ZATCA's platform tracks the chain per **taxpayer**, a genesis PIH after existing history could be treated as a broken or second sequence (a prohibited function, Detailed Guideline §5.6) or produce a warning/rejection on the first submission.
- **`PIH = old solution's last hash`** rests on reading the chain as per **taxpayer**; if it is per unit, the new unit's first document would reference a hash from a different unit's sequence — which the Resolution's definition of a unit does not contemplate — and it also requires the old solution to hand over its final hash, which no text obliges.
- The accountant's statement matches the *per-unit* reading and ZATCA's SDK genesis value, and it is the more likely reading of Third/E (2); but "more likely" is not authority, and the owner's instruction is explicit: **do not encode a default**.

**Classification: ZATCA MIGRATION POLICY UNRESOLVED.** Not the accountant's to settle — it is a question for ZATCA (or a written answer from ZATCA's Fatoora support/developer channel, kept as evidence). **Exact question to submit to ZATCA:**

> "A VAT-registered taxpayer in the Integration (Phase 2) phase replaces its e-invoicing solution. The old solution's EGS unit(s) have submitted invoices with a continuous hash chain and counter. The taxpayer onboards the new solution as a new EGS unit and obtains a new CSID. For the **first** electronic invoice or note generated by the new unit: (1) must the Previous Invoice Hash (KSA-13) be the initial value (Base64 of the SHA-256 hash of '0'), or must it be the hash of the last document generated by the old solution? (2) Must the Invoice Counter Value (KSA-16) restart at 1 for the new unit, or continue from the old unit's last value? **Consequence for Batch 1C:** none for the migration entries themselves — opening items are not electronic invoices and never enter a chain (§14.1). The unresolved point bites at the **first live e-invoice of a migrated Phase-2 taxpayer**, which is an onboarding (M12) matter. Until answered: the platform keeps its current, SDK-sourced behaviour (genesis PIH for the first document of a company's own unit) for **new** taxpayers; for a **migrated** Phase-2 taxpayer the onboarding step must **stop and show this unresolved item** rather than silently start a chain — a fail-closed gate, not a default. `SAUDI LEDGER PRODUCT DECISION` (the gate), `ZATCA MIGRATION POLICY UNRESOLVED` (the value).

### C. Historical VAT — VERIFIED

The chain *historical invoice → original VAT event → already reported in the old system* and the non-event *migration into Saudi Ledger* are established by VAT Law Arts. 1, 2, 18 and 24, VAT IR Art. 20, Art. 53(1) and the Tax Returns article para (2), read in §14.1.2–14.1.3. **When Saudi Ledger creates a new VAT accounting event:** only on the platform's own taxable events — a supply it invoices (Art. 53(1)(a)(1)), an advance it receives (53(1)(a)(2)), a credit/debit note it issues (Art. 54, Art. 40(1)–(6)), or an Art. 40(10)–(11) adjustment on an unpaid bill (§15.2 A). **Never** on: importing an open item, allocating a receipt to a migrated item, applying a migrated credit, or clearing OBE. The **cutover VAT balances** (output VAT payable, input VAT receivable, net position with ZATCA) are imported as opening balance-sheet balances of the previous returns, reconciled to the last filed return (§15.6 R1/R9). **A previously unreported supply** is corrected in the old return under Art. 63 (VAT Law Art. 24(3), 25) — a tax workflow, not a migration step. `AUTHORITATIVE REQUIREMENT`. Confirms A1-AR.

## 15.4 Chart-of-accounts migration design — `SAUDI LEDGER PRODUCT DECISION`

**Can the old chart be imported directly?** No — three properties of this platform forbid a replacement: (1) services resolve accounts by **role** (`system_code`) inside the posting seam, and those system accounts are created by the org-seed trigger and protected; (2) there is **one** AR and one AP control account per organisation by design (the party is on the line), so several old receivable accounts cannot each become a control account; (3) cash lives on **one leaf per bank account**, created with the bank account (D-3), never on an imported account. What ERPNext's importer does — replace the chart — would delete the very accounts the seam needs. **A mapping layer is required.**

**The mapping model (conceptual; no schema now):**
- `migration_chart_rows` — one row per old account: `source_code`, `source_name`, `source_name_ar`, `source_parent_code`, `source_type` (as the old system named it), `source_is_group`, `source_balance_dr/cr` (from the TB file, joined by code), `content_hash`; identity `(company, batch, source_code)`.
- Each row gets exactly one **mapping decision**, stored and audited: `map_to_system` (→ a system account by role: `AR`, `AP`, `VAT_OUTPUT`, `VAT_INPUT`, `CUSTOMER_DEPOSITS`, `CUSTOMER_CREDITS`, `SALES`, `PURCHASES`, `SALARIES`, `SALARIES_PAYABLE`, `GOSI_*`, `EXTERNAL_TRANSFERS`, `RETAINED_EARNINGS` (new), `OPENING_BALANCE_EQUITY` (new; never a mapping target — refused)), `map_to_bank` (→ a Saudi Ledger bank account's leaf, existing or created in the migration's bank step — never the `CASH` header), `create` (a new posting or header category under a mapped parent, with the old name/Arabic name and type derived from the old root), `merge_into` (an existing non-system category), or `skip` (allowed only when the old balance is **zero** and no open item references it; reason required). **Unmapped rows block** the opening TB step.
- **Type mapping:** the old root type (asset/liability/equity/income/expense, however spelled) → `categories.type`; anything the file cannot classify is a blocking error, never a guess. **Header/posting:** an old group with children → a header (`is_posting = false`); an old group *with a balance of its own* is an error (a header cannot carry a balance here) — the balance must be moved to a posting child in the file.
- **Control accounts:** every old receivable account maps to `AR`, every old payable account to `AP` — **mandatory** (`map_to_system` is the only decision offered for rows the file marks as receivable/payable, or that the opening AR/AP item files reference). Their opening balances are **not** imported as TB lines; they are **derived** from the open items (§15.6 R2/R3) and the TB line for the control account is compared to that derivation. A difference is a blocking reconciliation error, not a plug.
- **VAT accounts:** map to `VAT_OUTPUT` / `VAT_INPUT`; a net "VAT settlement / due to ZATCA" account maps to a **created** liability (or asset) category under the VAT header — the platform has no such system account today; the accountant's chart decides where the net position sits (a routine mapping choice, not a policy question).
- **Bank accounts:** each old bank/cash account → `map_to_bank`; the bank's opening balance is R4's input; **cash on hand** maps to a created posting leaf under `CASH` (D-3 allows a cash-on-hand leaf that is not a bank).
- **Equity and retained earnings:** capital/owner accounts → `create` under equity; the old "retained earnings" / "accumulated profit" → `RETAINED_EARNINGS` (new system account, equity, postable by the opening journal and the OBE clearing journal, and by year-end closing once that exists); the old system's **current-year result** is not an account to map — under A2 it arrives as **P&L account balances** (the P&L rows map to created/system income and expense accounts) and the balance sheet shows it as the current-period result.
- **Codes:** the old `source_code` is preserved on the mapping row and — because codes are how accountants read a chart and how the TB file joins — on the category as a new nullable `account_code` (unique per organisation when present; system accounts keep theirs blank unless mapped). Schema change for the build, not now.
- **Duplicates:** `(company, batch, source_code)` unique; a second row with the same normalised name and type in one batch is a warning that must be acknowledged; two rows mapping to the same **system** account is allowed (their balances aggregate; the TB reconciliation is per *target* account); two rows mapping to the same **created** account is `merge_into` and must be explicit.
- **Mandatory system accounts:** the org-seed trigger has already created them; the importer never creates or deletes a system account; a mapping that leaves a role unmapped is fine (the role exists regardless) — but a role with an old-chart balance that is *not* mapped blocks (the balance would have nowhere to land).
- **Names:** old names are kept on created accounts; system accounts keep their platform names (renaming is allowed by the existing rule, but the migration does not rename — the accountant can, after).
- **Order:** chart mapping is **step 1** of the workflow (§15.5) because every later file (TB, AR items, AP items, banks) references old account codes through it.

  ↓  5. Historical AP open items → `opening` bill rows (same; the 12-month clock runs from the original date)
  ↓  6. Bank accounts + opening balances per bank (D-3 leaf; statement evidence attached)

*(Amended 2026-09-20 per A5: the former step 8's "OPENING_BALANCE_EQUITY on the far side" and step 11's "Clear OBE → RETAINED_EARNINGS" journal are withdrawn — there is no balancing account and no clearing workflow. **Three streams, kept distinguishable by construction:** *historical information* = the previous system's records (not imported as transactions; referenced by source ids on items, deposits and the mapping); *opening position* = the one opening journal (`source = 'opening'`, dated cutover − 1, the only entry allowed to carry `is_opening`-like status) plus the `opening` items and deposits it explains; *current transactions* = everything dated on or after cutover through the ordinary writers. Reports show the opening journal as the first line of every account; the trial balance's opening column (G21) is the opening journal by construction; the income statement for the cutover year shows the imported YTD balances as the opening movement of each P&L account, dated cutover − 1, so a date filter from cutover shows the post-migration result alone — both views (§14.2.2) without a second ledger.

**Safety:** validation before any write (every check above runs on the preview against zero writes); duplicate detection by identity, refused not skipped; dry-run → preview → commit inside one transaction that re-computes the preview and refuses on any difference (the `cash_cutover_runs` pattern); before posting, a draft batch can be discarded entirely; after posting, cancellation = reversal of the opening journal through the seam + **marking** of the `opening` items and deposits as reversed (they are kept; their links to staging stay), recorded as a new audited act — nothing deleted (A4; the Phase 3 build's "unlink and remove" was the pre-A4 reading and is corrected in §16.12); audit trail on the batch (who, when, company, source system, files and their hashes, per-row source ids, mapping decisions with actor, validation results, the reconciliation, the journal ids, the clearing journal, any reversal); retries are safe because identity, not position in a file, is the key. A failed commit rolls back the transaction whole — no partial opening state can exist.

# 16. Migration Correction Policy — Definitive Research (2026-09-19)

**Scope.** Two questions the owner posed before the Batch 1C UI: (Q1) when a committed migration is later found wrong, must Saudi Ledger preserve the migrated records and correct, or may it reverse, remove and reconstruct; (Q2) when an imported opening position does not balance, may an OBE residual ever be permitted. Plus (Q3) one sentence on shared master data across companies. **Order of reasoning:** what the Saudi authoritative sources require first; how Saudi Ledger implements it second. **Source rule applied:** SOCPA and ZATCA primary texts only, each read in the actual document with page or article cited; one Saudi statute outside that pair (the Commercial Books Law, official Bureau of Experts text) is reported because it is the primary Saudi law on bookkeeping integrity and omitting it would be an absence a reader cannot detect — it is labelled as such. Odoo and ERPNext are implementation comparison only. Nothing below was taken from a blog, a firm, a forum or a summary.

**Status of the current design after this research (one paragraph, the rest is evidence):** the current reversal (remove opening items and deposits, re-run) is **NOT supported** as a permanent policy by the primary sources — no source names ERP migration, and no source authorises deleting an accounting record that entered the books; the sources that exist all point the other way (correction by a subsequent entry that leaves the original visible; every entry corroborated; tamper-controlled electronic records; ZATCA's own model of "cancel = note + re-issue, never delete" for its documents). The **GL half** of the current design (mirror the opening journal, keep both) is consistent with them. The **subledger half** (delete the opening invoices, bills and deposit rows) is not, and should change to Policy C (§16.7). The **OBE residual** design has **no primary-source basis either way** — §16.9 escalates it. Q3 is answered in §16.10.

## 16.3 What is NOT established — explicit gaps

1. **No SOCPA or ZATCA source addresses migration between accounting systems.** Not opening balances at a cutover, not the carrying of open items from a previous system, not the correction of a migrated position. Stated plainly: the sources do not answer Q1 for migration *as such*; they answer it for accounting records in general, from which the migration case has to be **reasoned**, and that reasoning is marked as reasoning below.
2. **No SOCPA or ZATCA source states an entry-level rule "a posted record must be reversed, not deleted".** Art. 66(3)(c)/(e)/(f) and IAS 8 point that way (see §16.6); none says it in those words. The Saudi text that does say it (the Commercial Books Implementing Regulation) was not obtainable officially.
3. **No source distinguishes a "subledger record" (an open invoice row) from a "journal entry"** for correction purposes. The distinction Saudi Ledger's current reversal relies on — mirror the journal but delete the subledger rows — has no basis in any text; nor does the opposite.
4. **No source addresses an unbalanced opening position, a temporary balancing equity account, or any threshold or permission for a residual** (Q2). IFRS's definition of equity as the residual interest (the Conceptual Framework, ¶4.63, not re-fetched in this pass) and Zakat Art. 20 make "the difference is equity" a tautology, not a permission; which *component* of equity it is remains the accountant's classification (Art. 9 lets ZATCA amend classification).
5. **The Arabic texts prevail** for Z1–Z4 and were not read; the E-Invoicing Regulation itself (Z5) was not obtainable; the SOCPA-hosted endorsed IAS 8 text is behind a login.

- **Deletion**: Frappe refuses to delete a submitted document ("Submitted Record cannot be deleted. You must Cancel it first", delete_doc.py L315–); a *cancelled* document may then be deleted by a user with the delete permission, and `Accounts Settings.delete_linked_ledger_entries` ("Delete Accounting and Stock Ledger entries on deletion of transaction", L273) decides whether the GL rows go with it — deletion of cancelled accounting documents is an **opt-in, permissioned exception**, not the default.
- **Correction = amend**: cancelling and amending a submitted document produces a new document named `<original>-1`, `-2`, … (`naming.py` `_set_amended_name`, L585–596; `amended_from` links it) — the original number stays occupied by the cancelled document and the correction carries a **suffix**. This is the document-number consequence the owner asked about, as ERPNext lives with it.
- **Reading for Q1**: ERPNext's default is *cancel by mirror, keep the original, amend with a suffixed number*; deletion of a cancelled document is possible only by explicit setting and permission. Its opening invoices are ordinary (non-tax) invoices distinguished by `is_opening`, subject to the same cancel/amend discipline.

## 16.6 Accounting conclusion, supported by the Saudi primary sources

Stated as the sources support it, with the reasoning step marked.

1. **A record that entered the accounting books is a record ZATCA may examine, must remain corroborated, and must be protected from tampering** (Z1 Art. 66(1), (3)(c)(e)(f)). The books must state the financial position accurately and allow the entries to be reviewed (Commercial Books Law Arts. 1, 6 — outside the pair, official text). — *Established.*
2. **Errors are corrected, not made to disappear**: IAS 8 as endorsed by SOCPA (S1, S3) and applied by SOCPA (S2) corrects by restatement in the statements; ZATCA's own model for its documents is "the original stays; a referencing document corrects it; nothing is cancelled or deleted in place" (Z2 Third/C(2), Annex 1; Z3 p. 27, p. 63). — *Established for financial statements and for e-invoices respectively.*
3. **Reasoning step (marked):** an opening invoice, bill or deposit created by the commit **entered the books** — it backed the AR/AP/deposit control, it appeared on the customer's statement and ageing, it was the corroboration (Art. 66(3)(c)) of the party lines of the opening journal. Removing it afterwards changes what the books said at the opening date without leaving a trace in the books themselves (the trace survives only in staging rows and the audit log). No text names this act; but of the two readings — "an authorised, audited deletion of a non-e-invoice record is permitted because no text forbids it" versus "the electronic accounting records must be protected from being altered after the fact and their corroboration kept" — the second is the one Art. 66(3)(f) is written to serve, the one IAS 8's restatement model embodies, and the one ZATCA applies to the documents it does regulate. The first reading rests on an absence.
4. **Therefore, as a matter of Saudi accounting treatment:** a migration correction should **preserve every record that entered the ledger** (the opening journal — already preserved by the mirror — and the opening subledger rows) and correct by **subsequent, dated, referencing records**; **reconstruction** (delete and re-create) is defensible only for **staging and non-posted** state, which never entered the books. That is **Policy C**. Policy A (the current design) is not supported by the sources; Policy B (preserve and correct only, never reconstruct anything) goes further than the sources require for un-posted staging.
5. **Where this conclusion is a reading rather than a text**, it is item 3 above — and that is exactly the question to put to the accountant (§16.9), not to decide by engineering preference.
6. **The ZATCA boundary (Q1-D):** ZATCA's e-invoicing rules (Z2, Z3) do **not** reach a migrated open item — it is not a document the solution generated, carries no ICV, hash, QR or e-invoice record, and never enters the chain; R7 stays exactly as built. But "not an e-invoice" removes only the e-invoicing prohibition; it does **not** remove Art. 66's record obligations, which apply to the accounting books as such. The two corrections are different things: an **e-invoice** is corrected by a credit/debit note (a new ZATCA document); an **accounting record** is corrected by a subsequent entry/record in the books. A migrated open item needs the second, never the first.
7. **On the OBE residual (Q2):** the sources establish that equity is the residual of assets over liabilities (Zakat Art. 20; the IFRS Framework) and that classification within equity is a matter of SOCPA standards ZATCA may amend (Art. 9). They establish **no rule** about accepting an unbalanced imported position, no threshold, and no temporary account. The current design's declaration mechanism is neither supported nor contradicted — it is a product construction and is escalated (§16.9).

# 16.12 The correction policy as DECIDED — invariants A4 and A5 (2026-09-20)

## 16.12.1 Invariant A4 — a committed migration's accounting records are never deleted

`AUTHORITATIVE REQUIREMENT` by the accountant's reading of IAS 8 as endorsed by SOCPA and of ZATCA's record obligations (§16.1–§16.2); `ACCOUNTANT DECISION — RECORDED`.

1. **Staging is not the ledger.** A batch that has not committed may be re-imported, corrected, rebuilt, revalidated or discarded freely — nothing it holds has entered the books. *(Unchanged.)*
2. **Committed accounting data is never deleted.** The opening journal, the opening invoices and bills, and the migrated deposit payments a commit created **stay in the books forever**. No path — the migration reversal included — deletes them. *(Was violated: the Phase 3 reversal deleted the opening invoices, bills and deposit rows.)*
3. **The reversal is a marked, referencing act.** The opening journal is mirrored through `postJournalEntry` (`source = 'opening_reversal'`, `reversal_of`) and both entries stay in the books *(unchanged)*; each opening invoice/bill is **marked reversed** — a column pair (`reversed_at`, `reversed_by_migration_batch_id`) permitted only on `is_opening` rows and written only by the migration's reversal (CHECK + trigger) — and each migrated deposit is reversed by a **superseding record** (`migration_deposit_reversals`), because `payments` is append-only (UPDATE and DELETE revoked; the 0079 DELETE grant and its row trigger are withdrawn). Reversed rows keep their number, dates, party, amount, `migration_open_item_id` and every audit row.
4. **A replacement is a separate record with a NEW Saudi Ledger number.** The corrected re-run (a new batch, same source ids) creates its opening items **`OPEN-<batch>-<seq>`** (the replacement batch's id and the item's ordinal within it), never the original number and never a number derived from the source number. `invoices_company_number_unq` / `bills_company_number_unq` stay **full** unique indexes. The prefix `OPEN-` is **reserved**: an invoice or bill number of that shape supplied by any other path is refused (`reserved_document_number`), so a customer cannot type a colliding number later.
5. **The original source number is provenance, verbatim.** `migration_open_items.document_number` holds the previous system's number unchanged in every batch; a new `ledger_document_number` on the staging row records what the ledger row was actually called (the source number for a first migration, `OPEN-…` for a replacement). The reversed row itself still carries the original number.
6. **Provenance links both ways.** `invoices.replaces_invoice_id`, `bills.replaces_bill_id`, `payments.replaces_payment_id` point from the replacement at the reversed original (opening-only by CHECK); `migration_batches.replaces_batch_id` points from the replacement batch at the reversed batch. A staging row's `resolved_*` link **stays** pointing at the reversed row — the 0079 "unlink-only" trigger exception is withdrawn; committed staging is immutable again without exception.
7. **Readers exclude reversed rows through one shared predicate per table**, and a **sweep test** — written red first, proven to fail when a reader omits the predicate — guards every AR/AP/deposit/ageing/statement/list reader. A reversed opening item contributes nothing to any balance and appears in the statement only as a zero-effect event.
8. **The blockers stay.** A reversal is refused while any opening item has an allocation, credit note, payment or credited amount, while any deposit has an allocation or refund, or while the opening month was closed by someone else. *(Unchanged; see §16.12.5.)*

## 16.12.2 Invariant A5 — an unbalanced opening position is a migration failure

`ACCOUNTANT DECISION — RECORDED`; the accountant's reading of IAS 8 / SOCPA on proper classification.

1. **`CHART_BALANCED` fails whenever Σ Dr ≠ Σ Cr over the mapped rows** — always, with no declaration that turns it into a warning. The failing check names the amount and side and tells the operator what to do: complete the file — add the row(s) that carry the difference, mapped or created to the account the **cause** dictates (a created capital account, `RETAINED_EARNINGS` when the source position genuinely is retained earnings, or the corrected asset/liability row). The product never chooses the account.
2. **The opening journal carries no balancing-account line.** There is **no `OPENING_BALANCE_EQUITY` account** — removed from `system_account_templates`, from every organisation's `categories`, from `MIGRATION_ONLY_SYSTEM_CODES`, from the posting seam and from the line trigger (owner: removed, not tombstoned — "an account that exists but can never be posted to is a shape waiting for someone to find a use for it"). `RETAINED_EARNINGS` stays, as a mapping target only.
3. **Commit refuses a non-zero residual unconditionally**, and **R6 is a blocking gate**: every posted line lands on a named account and Dr = Cr on those lines alone.
4. **There is no clearing workflow.** `POST …/clear-obe`, `clearing_journal_entry_id`, `source = 'opening_clearing'` and `obe_residual_reason` are removed. Nothing classifies a difference after commit, and nothing classifies it automatically before.
5. **No dev/test residual is migrated silently.** The schema migration that removes the account refuses to run while any journal line references it; the only such rows were test fixtures, handled inside the test rewrite.

## 16.13.4 Issue 2 — the partly-settled opening item (research; no answer chosen)

**What the primary sources establish (each read in the pack's §14–§16 passes; cited there):** IAS 8 ¶41–42 as endorsed by SOCPA — prior-period errors are corrected by restatement and disclosure in the financial statements; nothing at the entry or subledger level. VAT IR Art. 66(3)(c)(e)(f) — every entry stays corroborated, data entry and processing are documented, electronic records are protected from tampering. ZATCA E-Invoicing Resolution Third/C(2), Annex 1 and Guideline §5.5/p. 63 — generated e-invoices are never deleted or altered; correction is a referencing document. VAT IR Art. 54 — credit/debit notes reference the original tax invoice (previously verified for §14.1). Zakat Regs Arts. 9, 20, 23(1) — classification per SOCPA standards; not on this question. **Not established by the sources reviewed:** any rule on how a partly-settled receivable is corrected at the subledger level — whether the settlement follows the corrected item or the corrected item is a new record; whether a migration data error is an IAS 8 "error" at all when the previous system's statements were right and only the transferred figure was wrong; whether a Saudi Ledger credit note may reference a document issued by a previous solution.

**Two things the question must keep apart.** (i) A **migration data error** — the old invoice was for 90,000 and 100,000 was transferred (the supply and its VAT were correct in the old system; nothing about the supply changes); (ii) a **commercial adjustment** of the historical supply — the old invoice really was 100,000 and the customer is now owed 10,000 off it, which is a change of consideration (a credit-note event under Art. 40/54, and one whose referencing document would name an invoice outside Saudi Ledger's chain — §16.13.1 #12). Option A/B below concern (i); (ii) is a different decision and is flagged as such.

**Option A — adjust the outstanding on the existing item.** Two shapes exist: A1 edit the stored amount in place (excluded by A4 — a committed accounting record is never edited; the row is frozen once reversed and `update` refuses non-drafts today); A2 an **appended, dated correction record** referencing the item (an "opening adjustment", non-tax) that reduces its outstanding and posts Dr equity-side / Cr AR(party) for 10,000 through the seam. Audit trail: original 100,000 stays, the adjustment is a new row with its reason; source fidelity: the staging row still says 100,000 and the item's number is unchanged; allocation history: the 40,000 allocation stays exactly as recorded; ageing and statement: outstanding 60,000 → 50,000 from the adjustment's date, the item keeps its original date and ageing; reconciliation: subledger (100,000 − 40,000 − 10,000) = GL AR(party) if the adjustment posts to AR — one more component readers must know about (the same reader-sweep class as §16.12); VAT: none; explainability: "item as migrated, payment, migration correction −10,000"; interaction with §16.12: the batch stays `committed`; the correction is a per-item record, not a batch reversal — a new concept, not in the repository today.

**Option B — reverse and replace, then reallocate.** The whole batch or the item is reversed (mirror journal), the item marked reversed, a replacement `OPEN-<batch>-<seq>` for 90,000 created with provenance, and the 40,000 allocation moved: today the reversal is **refused** while the allocation exists, so B needs the existing D-4 `unallocate` (an append-only correction row, `payment_allocation_reversals`) followed by `allocate` to the replacement — both mechanisms exist and keep history. Audit trail: original, reversal, replacement, unallocation, reallocation — five records, all referencing; source fidelity: the replacement's staging row carries the corrected 90,000 and the original number as provenance; allocation history: the 40,000 is visible twice (against the original, reversed; against the replacement); ageing: the replacement keeps the original issue/due dates — ageing unchanged; statement: shows the reversal and the replacement as events (today reversed rows are excluded from the statement — a display decision); reconciliation: R2 holds on the replacement batch by construction; VAT: none; explainability: more records, each self-describing; interaction with §16.12: **this is the model already built**, extended from batch-level to item-level plus the reallocation step.

**Odoo (`addons/account`, master @ `c55c82d`) — implementation comparison only.** Correcting a posted invoice's amount means *reset to draft* (`account_move.py` `button_draft` ~L6269; `_check_draftable` ~L6348 refuses hashed/locked moves; `l10n_sa_edi/models/account_move.py` L178–183 refuses once a validated ZATCA document exists in production) and editing the line — and `account_move_line.py` `write` (~L1860–1878) **unreconciles any line whose reconciled fields change** (`lines_to_unreconcile.remove_move_reconcile()`), so the payment's partial reconciliation is dropped and must be re-matched: even Odoo's edit-in-place detaches the settlement. The alternative is the reversal wizard (`wizard/account_move_reversal.py` `reverse_moves` L110–175; `modify_moves` L179): `_reverse_moves(cancel=True)` (`account_move.py` L5495–5540) removes the original's reconciliations, creates the mirror with `reversed_entry_id`, and "modify" copies a fresh draft — Option B's shape, with reconciliation redone afterwards. The opening move cannot be changed once posted (`company.py` `_update_opening_move` L865–880). Odoo has no Option A2 concept for a receivable; a partial refund (credit note) is its amount-reduction path.

**ERPNext (`erpnext/accounts`, `develop` @ `db6e089`) — implementation comparison only.** An invoice's outstanding is derived, not stored: `utils.py` `get_voucher_outstandings` (~L2355–2420) sums Payment Ledger Entry rows `against_voucher_no` with `delinked = 0`. So a Journal Entry with `reference_type = Sales Invoice` (`journal_entry.py` `validate_entries_for_advance` ~L530–560) reduces an invoice's outstanding **without editing the invoice** — an appended record: Option A2's shape exists there as an ordinary mechanism. Cancel+amend (Option B): `controllers/accounts_controller.py` L1196–1205 — on cancel of an invoice, if Accounts Settings `unlink_payment_on_cancellation_of_invoice` (default `1`, `accounts_settings.json` L156) the linked Payment Entry references are **unlinked** (`utils.py` `unlink_ref_doc_from_payment_entries` L1035, `remove_ref_doc_link_from_pe` L1084–1160: allocations set to 0, the payment's `unallocated_amount` restored, or "Please cancel payment entry manually first" when it cannot); the amended invoice gets a suffixed name (`amended_from`, `sales_invoice.json` L432) and the payment is re-reconciled through Payment Reconciliation. With `enable_immutable_ledger` (`accounts_settings.json` L485–489) cancellations post dated reversal entries and reports keep them. Opening invoices are ordinary invoices with `is_opening = Yes` against the Temporary Opening account (`opening_invoice_creation_tool.py` L132–143, L340) and follow the same discipline.

**Where the two converge / differ.** Both keep every posted record and correct by referencing records (never delete). Both detach the existing settlement when the original is reversed/reset and re-attach it to the corrected document (Option B mechanics). ERPNext additionally offers an appended adjustment against the original (Option A2 mechanics); Odoo does not. Convergence between two products is evidence about software, not an accounting requirement, and neither product is a Saudi authority.

## 16.14.1 The consequential finding first — a credit note against an invoice the previous system issued (Question 3)

**What the sources establish.**

- The obligation to credit attaches to the **taxable person**, and the trigger is an Art. 40(1) event **"after a Tax Invoice has been issued"** — not "after this solution issued it". VAT IR Art. 54(1)–(2) (retrieved PDF p. 50; footnotes 30–31: amended 09/11/2021G). A "Tax Invoice" is any invoice issued in accordance with Art. 53 (Art. 53(11), p. 50) — nothing restricts it to an electronic one or to the current system.
- What the note must carry: all Art. 53 information relating to the invoice's nature, a **"reference to the sequential number of the Tax Invoice issued in respect of the initial Supply"** and **"a clear reference –as specified by the Authority– to previously issued Tax Invoice(s)"** (Art. 54(4), pp. 50–51, footnote 33; the printed English of ¶4 is itself garbled mid-sentence — the Arabic prevails).
- How the Authority specified that reference: Resolution Annex 2 field **13.1 "A reference to the original invoice(s) that the credit/debit note is related to"** (Annex 2, p. 32 standard and the simplified table following); Detailed Guideline v2 §4.3 p. 19: *"The reference fields can be used to indicate the Invoice Reference Number(s) of Original Invoice(s)… taxpayers can provide Invoice Reference Numbers as a range"*; Technical Guideline v2 FAQ p. 72: *"Billing Reference ID is the link to Original Invoices… As per Article 54… This is not a new requirement, it existed since 1 Jan 2018."* The reference is the original's **invoice number, as text** — not its UUID, not its hash, not membership of a chain.
- The chain is a property of the **issuing unit**, not of the referenced invoice: *"The Hash of the Electronic Invoice is then embedded in the next Electronic Invoice in the sequence"* (Resolution Third/C(5), pp. 3–4); *"must not be able to generate more than one sequence… through each of the units"* (Third/E(2), p. 4); Detailed Guideline p. 27/34: all invoices generated by an E-Invoice Solution Unit are linked by PIH into a single chain; Technical Guideline FAQ pp. 76–77: after a rejection *"Previous Invoice Hash will be based on immediately preceding document"*. A credit note issued by Saudi Ledger therefore enters **Saudi Ledger's** sequence with its own UUID, ICV and PIH (Third/C(3),(5),(7)); the referenced original is named, not linked.
- The VAT effect of such a note is a **current-period** adjustment: the supplier reduces output tax *"in the Tax Return for the Tax Period in which the event… occurred or… during which the Credit Note was issued… whichever is later"* (Art. 40(5), p. 29); the customer corrects input tax in the period the note is issued (Art. 40(6)).

**What is NOT established by the sources reviewed.** (i) Any ZATCA text that names a *change of e-invoicing solution* or an original issued by a *previous* solution — silence, not a rule. (ii) Whether the FATOORA platform cross-checks field 13.1 against invoices it has previously received; the XML Implementation Standard (the business rules) **could not be retrieved this pass** (the zatca.gov.sa document URLs returned HTML). The Detailed Guideline's explicit allowance of a *range* of IRNs in the text field (p. 19) is hard to reconcile with a per-invoice cross-check, but that is reasoning, not text. (iii) The parenthetical in Detailed Guideline §4.3 — notes are issued "for a Tax Invoice / Simplified Tax Invoice (after an e-invoice has been issued)" — is ambiguous and is not restated as a condition anywhere else reviewed.

**Practical consequence, stated plainly.** On the texts as read, a migrated business credits an invoice its old system issued by issuing the credit note **from Saudi Ledger**: electronic, in Saudi Ledger's own sequence, carrying the old invoice's number (and date) in the reference field, reported to ZATCA as a new document, with the VAT reduction in the current return. The old invoice does **not** need to be in Saudi Ledger's chain, and nothing reviewed requires re-issuing it. What Saudi Ledger would need from the migration is the old invoice's **number and issue date** — which the opening item already preserves — and, for the buyer's input-tax correction, the buyer's identity. The residual uncertainty is (ii) above: whether ZATCA's platform validates the referenced number. That is the same underlying question as the two already open with ZATCA (§15.2 B — the first PIH after a change of solution; §15.2 D — a `PrepaidAmount` referencing an advance invoice issued by another solution, where Detailed Guideline §8(b) pp. 39–40 says the advance invoice's UUID is *"currently optional but it shall be mandated in future"*): **how ZATCA treats documents issued by a previous solution.** Per the owner's instruction this question is **folded into that enquiry as its third item** (§16.14.9), not kept as a separate thread.

**Not every migration discrepancy is an IAS 8 error**: only one that reached issued financial statements is. The three §16.13.5 cases are re-stated in §16.14.7.

## 16.14.3 The partly-settled opening receivable (Question 2)

**Not established by the sources reviewed** — none of editing the receivable, appending an adjustment, reversing it, replacing it, preserving or re-allocating the settlement, keeping or replacing the number. What the sources *constrain*: every entry corroborated by original documents (Art. 66(3)(c)), data entry and processing documented (66(3)(e)), tamper controls reviewable by the Authority (66(3)(f)), records orderly enough to review the entries and verify profit and loss (Commercial Books Law Art. 6), entries presumed made with the merchant's knowledge and consent (Art. 9), all financial operations recorded in the journal day by day (Art. 3). An append-only correction (A2) and a reversal-with-replacement (B) both satisfy these; an in-place edit of a posted amount (A1) is the one shape 66(3)(f) and Art. 9 are written against, and A4 already excludes it. The Commercial Books Implementing Regulation (Minister of Commerce Decision 699/1410H — the "correction by a new entry dated when discovered" rule quoted in practice) **could not be retrieved from an official source** (as in §16.2) and is not relied on.

## 16.14.4 VAT / the historical tax point (Question 4)

- **Importing an opening receivable creates no tax event.** None of Art. 40(1)(a)–(d) occurs (no cancellation, alteration, consideration change or return); the supply was made and invoiced before migration. Established by the text of Art. 40(1) read against the facts, not by a migration-specific rule (there is none).
- **Paying a historical receivable after migration creates no tax event**, except where the supplier had claimed bad-debt relief under Art. 40(7) — then Art. 40(9): tax on the amount received becomes payable in the period of receipt *"and a new Tax invoice must be issued to reflect the additional amount received"* (p. 30). Migration must therefore know whether Art. 40(7) relief was ever taken on an open item — **not captured by the staging today** (a gap to record, not fix).
- **The buyer side**: input tax on a bill unpaid twelve months after the date of supply is reversed (Art. 40(10)) and restored on payment (40(11)) — already recorded (§15.2 A); it runs from the *original* supply date, which the opening bill preserves.
- **A migration correction (Category B/D) creates no tax event** — same reasoning as the import. **A commercial adjustment (Category C) does**: Art. 40(4)–(5) place the adjustment in the period of the event or of the credit note (the later), i.e. in Saudi Ledger's current return.
- The general tax-point rule (tax due at the earliest of supply, invoice, receipt) lives in the VAT Law; the Law's PDF **could not be retrieved this pass** (the zatca.gov.sa URLs tried returned HTML). Retrieved and confirmed: IR Art. 20 (specific circumstances, pp. 13–14, amended 03/10/2021G) and Art. 53(7)(b) ("earliest of the date of the supply and the date of" receipt, p. 48).

## 16.14.6 Numbering (Question 8)

- Tax invoices: *"a sequential number which uniquely identifies the Tax Invoice"* (Art. 53(5)(b), p. 47); e-invoices/notes additionally carry a UUID, a tamper-resistant counter that *"cannot be reset or reformatted"* (Resolution Third/C(3),(7); Annex 1 prohibits counter reset); the IRN *"is just another name for Invoice Sequence Number"* (Detailed Guideline p. 62); a rejected document is re-submitted *"just like a new invoice having its own Invoice Counter Value and Hash"* (p. 63).
- **Not established:** whether an IRN may ever be reused; anything about the numbering of non-e-invoice records, migrated records, or replacement records. **These rules apply to ZATCA e-invoices and notes only.** A4's "new number for a replacement" rests on the accountant's decision and the audit-trail principle, not on a Saudi numbering rule — and the `OPEN-<batch>-<seq>` shape is unconstrained by any source.

# 17. The migration follow-ups as built — identity, item-level correction, migrated relief, credit notes through Fatoora (2026-09-22)

## 17.1 The historical invoice's identity (answer 3, the precondition)

- **Staging** (`migration_open_items.einvoicing_status` ∈ cleared · reported · pre_einvoicing, `source_uuid`): AR only (a payable never carries one — refused at import); a cleared/reported document REQUIRES its UUID; empty means NOT STATED, which is not a guess. Three CHECKs pin it.
- **The ledger row** (`invoices.opening_einvoicing_status`, `opening_source_uuid`): written at commit from staging; for an item the migration did not describe, `PUT /migration/open-items/{itemId}/identity` records it ONCE — trigger `refuse_opening_identity_change` refuses any later change (`opening_identity_already_recorded`). The UUID is the previous solution's, verbatim; nothing here invents one.
- **The read** (`MigrationOpenItem`): before commit the staged identity; after commit the LIVE row's (`liveIdentityRecorded`), with `liveDocumentId/Number/Outstanding` and `corrections` — the row an operator acts on is the one the read shows.

## 17.2 The item-level correction (answer 5, inside A4)

`POST /migration/open-items/{itemId}/correct` — keyed on the STAGING row, the migrated document's identity across corrections:

1. The original ledger row STAYS, marked reversed by its own batch (`reversed_by_migration_batch_id`, the A4 marker) and carrying `opening_correction_journal_entry_id`; the Batch 1C trigger then freezes it.
2. A REPLACEMENT row — `OPEN-<batch>-<seq>`, the corrected amount, every fact of the original (dates, party, identity, relief) — links back through `replaces_invoice_id` / `replaces_bill_id` and keeps `migration_open_item_id`; the per-item uniqueness is now **one LIVE row per staged item** (the 0077 indexes narrowed to `reversed_at IS NULL`), and the A4 marker trigger admits the chain (a replacement is linked to its batch by the staging id, not only by `resolved_invoice_id`).
3. ONE entry, source `opening_correction`, dated the correction date (an open month on or after the opening date; a locked month fails closed with `period_closed`; earlier than the opening refused `opening_correction_before_opening`): a receivable decrease is **Dr Retained earnings / Cr AR (customer)**; an increase the reverse; payables mirror. The customer's AR sub-ledger moves by the delta; the opening journal is untouched.
4. Refused by name: `opening_item_partly_settled` — an item acted on since the migration (an allocation, a credit note, a payment) is still §16.12.5's open question; until the accountant answers, it is corrected by dated journals, not here. Also `opening_correction_no_change`, `migration_not_committed`, `opening_item_not_live` (the migration was reversed).
5. Audit: `opening_item_correct` with the original, the replacement, the delta, the reason, the entry and the staging item. Ledger invariants: a replacement's coverage is the `opening_correction` AR/AP line (`opening_correction_shape`).

A second correction chains on the replacement; three rows for one item are three rows — nothing is deleted.

## 17.3 The migrated bad-debt relief as a structured fact (Art. 40(7) → 40(9))

`historicalVat.badDebtReliefClaimed` was information only; it is now written at commit onto the opening receivable as `bad_debt_relief_source = 'migrated'` with `bad_debt_relief_claimed_on` / `bad_debt_relief_vat_amount` when the previous system recorded them (the CHECK admits a migrated relief without a journal entry and with a null date/amount; a relief RECORDED here still requires all three). ## 17.4 Credit notes against opening items, through Fatoora (answer 3)

`creditNotes.ts` no longer refuses an opening original as such. It refuses by name while the identity is unstated (`opening_item_einvoicing_identity_missing`) or a cleared/reported original lacks its UUID (`opening_item_source_uuid_missing`); a reversed original is refused earlier, at the shared opening-row write boundary (`opening_item_reversed`). Once identified, the note is an ordinary Saudi Ledger credit note (381, its own ICV, the chain) whose `BillingReference` names the PREVIOUS SYSTEM's document number (staging `document_number`, not the `OPEN-` ledger number); a pre-e-invoicing original needs only its number. The credit-notes picker offers an identified, live opening item and nothing else. **Reasoned, not verified:** whether ZATCA's validator accepts a BillingReference to a number outside our chain is exactly what the sandbox cannot attest (§16.14.1); the construction is validated locally and stands until the enquiry answers.
