# A3 — Recurring documents (DESIGN)

**Status: ✅ BUILT (drafts-only, as decided), backend and UI (`Recurring.tsx`). Current state authority: CLAUDE.md §2.**

The second half of the wedge. A1 removed typing on the **purchase** side; A3
removes it on the **sales** side — rent, retainers, subscriptions. *"I stopped
doing data entry"* is only half-true while you still recreate the same invoice
every month.

No provider, no model, no external dependency. The scheduler (M12.8) and the
approval workflow (M10) both already exist.

---

## 1. Both design questions turned out non-obvious

Neither answer is the one the automation spec assumed, and **one of them
contradicts what that spec says outright.** Recorded here rather than corrected
silently.

---

## 2. 🔴 Q1 — Does a generated invoice arrive as a draft, or does the rule's approval carry?

### The case for the rule's approval carrying is stronger than it first looks

There is real precedent in this codebase. M10.4's **self-approve-on-create**:
when a user with `approve` authority creates an invoice, it issues immediately —
`can(role, "invoices", "approve")` drives an `autoApprove` flag. The *act of
creation by an authorised person* already counts as approval here.

And the product argument is not weak: if every generated invoice must be
approved by hand, we have saved typing but not attention. A tenant with fifteen
monthly retainers still has fifteen decisions every month.

### But ZATCA changes the cost of being wrong

In Xero or QuickBooks an auto-issued invoice that turns out to be wrong is
embarrassing and editable. Here, **approval fires issuance**: it consumes an
**ICV**, takes a position in the **ZATCA hash chain**, mints a **QR**, posts
**AR**, and (once onboarded) **queues submission to ZATCA**. None of that is
reversible. Correcting it requires a **credit note** — a second legal document,
in the same chain, with its own number.

So the question is not "draft or not" in the abstract. It is: **is unattended
issuance of a legal tax document an acceptable default?** For a document
generated by a rule written possibly a year earlier, by someone who may have
left, for a customer who may have cancelled — no.

### Decision

**Default: the rule generates a DRAFT.** Nothing reaches the books, the chain or
ZATCA until a human approves.

**Opt-in: auto-issue, per rule, explicitly chosen, with the consequence stated
at the point of choosing** — that this issues a legal document unattended and
that correcting one needs a credit note. This is what Xero and QuickBooks both
do (draft vs "approve and send automatically"), and it puts the decision with
the person who knows whether their rent invoice ever varies.

🔴 **And one invariant, borrowed from M11.7's invitations: a rule can never
grant authority its creator does not hold.** Auto-issue is only offerable to a
user with `can(role, "invoices", "approve")`. A bookkeeper may create a rule;
that rule generates drafts, because a bookkeeper cannot approve. Otherwise the
rule becomes a privilege-escalation device — write a rule today, have it issue
invoices you could not issue yourself, forever.

**Also:** the authority is checked **at generation time, not only at creation**.
If the creator's role is later reduced, or their membership deactivated, the rule
stops auto-issuing and falls back to drafts. A rule is not a stored credential.

## 3. 🔴 Q2 — A rule that generates into a locked period

### The automation spec is WRONG about this, and both of its options are wrong

It says a rule *"skips a locked period rather than failing"*. Two problems:

**First, it cannot skip, because it cannot even generate.** `invoicesService.create`
calls `checkPeriodOpen(date)` — creating a **draft** dated into a closed period
already throws today. That is deliberate (the guard predates A3), so a rule
firing into a locked period throws at creation, before any A3 logic runs.

**Second, and worse: silently skipping is the wrong behaviour anyway.** A skipped
recurring invoice means **a customer was not billed**, and nothing says so. That
is precisely the quiet-neglect failure this project keeps naming — the ZATCA
outbox, the PCSID expiry, the overdue alarm. Revenue silently not invoiced is the
same shape and arguably worse, because there is no external party to eventually
complain.

### The three real options

| Option | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| **Skip** | 🔴 Silent revenue loss. Rejected. |
| **Re-date into the next open period** | The invoice happens, dated later. But that **moves revenue between VAT periods without asking** — a tax consequence chosen by a job. Rejected as a default. |
| **Fail the run, record why, surface it** | The user learns their rule could not fire and decides: re-date, or unlock the period. Nothing is decided on their behalf. |

### Decision

**Fail the run, record the reason, surface it as an exception.** The rule stays
active and retries on its next scheduled date; the *missed* occurrence is
recorded as a failed run with `period_locked` and shown in the rules list.

This needs a **run history** per rule (`recurring_runs`: scheduled date, outcome,
generated document id or error), which is worth having regardless — "did my rent
invoice go out?" is a question the user will ask.

**Why not just re-date:** a closed period usually means the books were closed
deliberately. Moving a January invoice into February changes which VAT return it
lands in. That is the tenant's decision, and an automation that makes it quietly
is worse than one that stops and asks.

## 4. What gets built

### Data

**`recurring_rules`** — tenant-scoped, RLS:
- `entity` (`invoice` | `bill`), `template` (jsonb — the document to generate),
  `customer_id` / `vendor_id`
- `frequency` (`monthly` | `quarterly` | `yearly`), `day_of_month`, `starts_on`,
  `ends_on` (nullable), `next_run_on`
- `auto_issue` (boolean, default **false**), `created_by`
- `status` (`active` | `paused`)

**`recurring_runs`** — one row per attempt: `rule_id`, `scheduled_for`,
`outcome` (`generated` | `issued` | `failed`), `document_id`, `error_code`,
`ran_at`. This is the answer to "did it go out?" and the surface for §3.

### Generation

A fourth job on the **M12.8 scheduler** (`recurring-documents`, daily). For each
active rule whose `next_run_on` has arrived:

1. Build the document from `template`.
2. Create it as a **draft** through `invoicesService.create` / `billsService.create`
   — **the same path a human uses**, so validation, tax categories, seller
   identity and period locks all behave identically. No second creation path.
3. If `auto_issue` **and** the creator still holds `approve`: approve it, which
   fires the existing issuance.
4. Record the run; advance `next_run_on`.
5. On failure: record the run with its reason, **do not advance** past the missed
   occurrence silently, leave the rule active.

**Idempotency:** `unique(rule_id, scheduled_for)` on `recurring_runs`. A job that
runs twice, or two instances running concurrently, cannot double-generate — the
same guarantee the archive sweep and renewal reminders use.

### Surfaces

- **On the invoice/bill itself:** *"Repeat this monthly"* — a rule is a property
  of the document it repeats, so it is created from the document, not from a
  separate section. (Structure decision: Automation has **no nav entry**.)
- **Settings → Recurring:** the list of rules with their next run and last
  outcome. A list, not a hub.
- **Failed runs are visible** in that list, and each says what to do.

---

## 5. What A3 must not foreclose (Q2 = SME first, firms later)

- Rules carry their own `company_id`; never "the org's first company".
- The generator is a function of **a rule**, not of a request — so running rules
  for many companies is a loop, not a rewrite.

---

## 6. Out of scope

Automatic emailing of generated invoices (the mailer is still a no-op — queue
**B1**), usage-based or metered billing, proration, and recurring **payments**
(a different thing: taking money, not raising a document).

---

## 7. Open question for the owner

**Q1 — should auto-issue be offered at all in the first version?**

The design above allows it, gated on the creator's authority and re-checked at
generation. The conservative alternative is **drafts only for v1**, with
auto-issue added once rules have run for a while and tenants trust them.

Given there are **no customers yet**, nobody is asking for auto-issue, and the
failure mode is an unattended legal document in a hash chain — there is a decent
case for shipping drafts-only and adding the opt-in later. It costs one boolean
to defer and it removes the riskiest behaviour from the first version.

I lean drafts-only for v1, but it is your call: it trades some of the "stop doing
work" promise for a safer default.
